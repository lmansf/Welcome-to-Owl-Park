# Owl facts for Which owl are you (OP-328)

Every fact the quiz shows, and every owl habit an answer rests on, with where it was checked. A new
fact or answer goes in only with a source here. Checked on 2026-11-11 unless marked.

The quiz's words are in `packages/shared/src/whichOwlBank.ts`. A fact line is about the real bird,
never the park's own story.

## The facts on the result screen

| Owl | Fact shown | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Barn owl | Barn owls can catch a mouse in total darkness using their ears alone. One ear sits a little higher than the other. | Barn Owl Trust, "Barn Owl adaptations" (https://barnowltrust.org.uk/barn-owl-facts/barn-owl-adaptations): prey caught "in total darkness by hearing alone"; ears "placed asymmetrically, one higher than the other". |
| Burrowing owl | Burrowing owls live in burrows in the ground. In Florida they usually dig their own. | All About Birds, Burrowing Owl life history (https://www.allaboutbirds.org/guide/Burrowing_Owl/lifehistory): in Florida and the Caribbean they "usually excavate their own burrows"; elsewhere mostly burrows other animals dug. |
| Snowy owl | Snowy owls have feathers on their feet, like warm slippers. | The Peregrine Fund, Snowy Owl (https://peregrinefund.org/explore-raptors-species/Snowy_Owl): the feathers on their legs and feet are more densely layered than other owls'. |
| Screech owl | Screech owls mostly trill and whinny instead of screeching. | All About Birds, Eastern Screech-Owl (https://www.allaboutbirds.org/guide/Eastern_Screech-Owl): listen at night "for the trills and whinnies of this vocal owl". |
| Owl of the tree (great horned owl) | Great horned owls often move into old nests that hawks or crows built. | All About Birds, Great Horned Owl life history (https://www.allaboutbirds.org/guide/Great_Horned_Owl/lifehistory): they "usually adopt a nest that was built by another species", usually red-tailed hawks, other hawks, crows, ravens, herons or squirrels. |

Old facts taken out (OP-328): "Barn owls hunt by sound alone and can hear a mouse under snow" (no
source here backs the under-snow part for barn owls), "Burrowing owls live underground, often in
burrows other animals dug" (not true in Florida), and the owl of the tree's park story.

## The about lines

| Owl | Claim | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Barn owl | Flies almost without a sound; the heart-shaped face sends sound toward the ears. | Barn Owl Trust, as above: "silent flight"; the facial disk "collects and directs sound toward the inner ears". |
| Burrowing owl | Hunts at all hours; bobs up and down. | All About Birds, as above: they "hunt at all hours of the day and night"; "disturbed owls bob jerkily up and down", and hunting owls bob too. |
| Snowy owl | Sits in one spot for hours; hunts in daylight in the Arctic summer. | The Peregrine Fund, as above: "spend a good part of their day sitting and waiting"; hunts "during the daytime, particularly in the summer when days are very long". |
| Screech owl | No bigger than a pint glass; well camouflaged in trees. | All About Birds, Eastern Screech-Owl: "no bigger than a pint glass"; "supremely camouflaged"; hides "in nooks and tree crannies". |
| Owl of the tree | Lives from deserts to cities; hoots to guard its home. | All About Birds, Great Horned Owl life history: nests in deserts, common in parks, suburbs "and even cities"; pairs "defend their territories with vigorous hooting". |

## The habits the answers rest on

| Owl | Habit | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Barn owl | Listens and hunts by sound; harsh screech, no hoot; lives in barns and old buildings, uses nest boxes; roosts somewhere hidden and quiet; eats mice. | All About Birds, Barn Owl (https://www.allaboutbirds.org/guide/Barn_Owl): locates prey "by sound alone"; "don't hoot ... harsh screeches"; "barns and other old, abandoned buildings"; "roosts in hidden, quiet places during the day". |
| Burrowing owl | Burrows, digging its own in Florida; lines the burrow with grass and feathers among other things; rests on the dirt mound by the burrow; eats beetles, crickets and mice; active by day. | All About Birds, Burrowing Owl life history. |
| Snowy owl | Nests on the ground on a slight rise for the view; waits a long time; eats lemmings; some fly far south in winter. | The Peregrine Fund, Snowy Owl: ground nests "slightly elevated" for "a good vantage point"; lemmings; in winter as far south as Texas and Oklahoma in some years. |
| Screech owl | Trills and whinnies; hides in nooks, tree cavities and nest boxes; camouflaged against trees. | All About Birds, Eastern Screech-Owl. |
| Great horned owl | Moves into old hawk and crow nests; deep hooting to guard its territory; stays on its territory; claps its bill and hisses at intruders. | All About Birds, Great Horned Owl life history: answers intruders "with bill-clapping, hisses, screams". |
| Every owl | Turns its head almost all the way round (up to about 270 degrees). | Johns Hopkins Medicine news release, 2013, on EurekAlert (https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/469762). |

Kept out because no source here backed them: owls perching on fence posts as a choice of their own,
burrowing owls copying a rattlesnake's rattle, a screech owl stretching tall to look like a branch,
and barn owls hunting under snow.
